#### The Product: The SUN Workstation


Combine:


Processor (10 MHZ 68010, 256k RAM, virtual MMU)

Graphics (1024 by 800)

Networking (Ethernet)

Software (UNIX)


Goal: make SUN Workstations THE standard graphics workstation,
capable of running standard graphics software in CAD, CAM, OA, etc.

Desiderata: High performance graphics per user leads to high productivity.
People are willing to buy that for CAD/engineering solutions.
That is, if the task solved warrants the capital investment.
Not text editing, not communication, not personal computing.

Kind of applications that really increase productivity
are extremely cycle intensive. LISP, SMALLTALK,
Sharing CPU cycles makes no sense for the productivity market.

The problem of the Star is exactly that the capital investment
per office worker (business professional) is hard to justify.
No trouble to justify a CAD workstation at $100k.
You can justify that cost over one year!

Goal: Have a CAD workstation for $15k a piece.
That is what Intel wants, but they need 500 thereof.

UNIX: rich environment for standard tasks, further:
*** more and more people already know it!!!

UNIX is a key to this market because it helps at adapting
the applications to the system.
Usually the schedule is very tight. Full control is needed.
UNIX gives you that control.
We will need to add UNIX mechanism that simplify the
task of porting system, such as version control,
e.g. PWW under UNIX III.

Offer 750 replacement as a productivity enhancement:
many people that buy 750s would rather buy 10 SUNs!

#### System Market


**Objective**

Put current SUN boards into a box and sell it as a graphics workstation system.
Target to OEMs, Universities, End-User.

**Market Size**

500 SUN workstations in 1982 at an ASP of $7000 for $3.5M sales.
2000 SUNs in 1983 at an ASP of $5000 for $10M.


**Opportunities**

SUN workstation offers an cost reduction migration (from DEC, VMS, to UNIX, 68000).
What software companies want is end-user market place.
System integrators that used DECs before.

CAD workstations: a typical in-house design automation workstation
costs MORE than a SUN workstation OEM.
Problem: CAD software is often tied to peculiar hardware.

**Problems**

Missing Pieces: UNIX, Language support.

SUN workstation needs UNIX to run user applications. UNIX needs disk.
High-performance dedicated disk doubles system cost.
Needs distributed UNIX for full cost-effectiveness.

Manufacturing: Only way to make profit is over volume.
Very price sensitive, little price flexibility.

**Cost of entry**

Design cost: $200k, Packaging: $100k, UNIX: $100k

Costeffective only for OEMs that need more than 1000 Workstations per year.

**Summary**

Key: Volume manufacturing, strong sales and marketing.

Reduce time to market for OEM. Offer complete UNIX and Language support.

Evolve a family of SUN workstations: reduced cost, higher performance.

Make it easy to customize SUN hardware for OEM's needs (Multibus).

#### Board Market


**Objective**

Market current SUN boards as board level components.

**Market Size**


			1982			1983

SUN Processor Board	1000	$2000	$2M	3000	$1500	$4.5M
SUN B&W Graphics Board	300	$1200	$420K	1000	$1000	$1M
SUN Color Graphics 	300	$2500	$750K	1000	$2000	$2M
SUN 3 MBit Ethernet	200	$1000	$200K	100	$1000	$100k
SUN 10 MBit Ethernet	300	$1000	$300K	1000	$1000	$1M


**Problems**

Market is squeezed from chip manufacturers on one side,
from system manufacturers on other side, and from
current board licensees on third side.

The value added of putting chips on boards is small.
In low volume end-user sales, high overhead on sales.
High volumes OEM sales are difficult to attract because of costs.
Any volume manufacturer really needs to build the boards in-house
to control cost.

Cost of entry is low: design cost: $100k, reverse engineering cost: $10 - $20k.
Customer gets complete design engineering as part of board.
Spend a lot of effort on good documentation that facilitates reverse engineering.
Copyright protection is limited and legal action against customers difficult.

**Competition**

Intel and DEC have boards sales of $120M each.
System houses support their systems: E.g. DEC-LSI/11s.
Semiconductor Houses: Concentrating on supporting their chips and busses:
E.g. Motorola and Versabus.


**Opportunities:**

"Systems on boards": suddenly boards are not parts of systems,
but complete systems in themselves. For example: 68000, 256k RAM, Ethernet.

"Base of installed systems": there must be at least 100,000 Multibusses out there.

"Computer Accessory Market": add new functions to existing systems.
E.g. Ethrnets and graphics to Unibusses.

"Industrial Control and similar markets": where cost is not critical
but configuration flexibility is the key.

"Proprietary Chips": fit unique systems on single boards.

"Horizontal Integration": use best chips on the market from competing manufacturers.

"Licensing": offer design license on royalties after customer buys 100 boards.

"International Sales": board companies sell one/half overseas.


**Summary**

Think **Systems** not **Boards**.

Combine Systems on a single board: 68000, 256k RAM, Ethernet.
Solve system integration for user.

Provide New Capabilities to extisting systems: Networking, Graphics.

Concentrate on two busses: Multibus and VME. Utilize same designs for both.

Maintain ASP range of $1,000 - $10,000 and volume of 1,000 - 10,000 boards a year.

Key: Good customer support, strong sales force, national distribution.
